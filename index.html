About

I am a graduate student at Hanyang University (ERICA), majoring in Nano-Optoelectronics, with a focus on display materials. My research centers on improving high-resolution quantum-dot electroluminescent (QD-EL) devices by combining photolithography-based QD pixel patterning with oxide insulating layers formed by atomic layer deposition (ALD) to suppress electrical crosstalk and enhance process stability.

I aim to contribute to Display R&D by connecting material properties, process conditions, and device performance through structured experimentation and data-driven analysis.

Experience Highlights

Process + Device Integration

  • Combined QD pixel patterning (photolithography) with ALD insulating layers to suppress electrical crosstalk and improve process stability.
  • Optimized ALD conditions (precursor / temperature / pulse-purge) to tune film quality and interfacial charge characteristics, improving current distribution and EL operation stability.

Data-Driven Problem Solving

  • Diagnosed pattern defects and emission degradation by structuring hypotheses (exposure, solvent compatibility, surface energy, thermal damage) and validating single-variable changes.
  • Designed a leakage-current metric based on c-AFM data and linked it to luminance/EQE to find an optimal thickness trade-off for insulating layers while maintaining pixel resolution and process compatibility.

Collaboration & Communication

  • Worked with an industry partner to analyze QD-EL pixel process defects using current density, SEM, and electrical data.
  • Reduced misalignment in interpretation by creating shared weekly progress sheets, measurement data templates, and standardized process-condition formats.

Broader Display Exposure

  • Completed a display bootcamp and broadened understanding of display fundamentals and applications.
  • Received an excellence award at a patent universiade related to a QD color-conversion structure using Micro-LED as a backlight.
